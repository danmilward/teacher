Edi App

Helping every learner become a confident writer

About

This app is an early prototype I made to help learners succeed in their structured literacy classes. It grew out of another app I made to help my son learn spelling in a fun, engaging way while we were away on a family trip. I hope it makes a difference to someone, somewhere :)

How to use it

  1. Tap Writing Practice and type one sentence on each line, or tap a sentence starter.
  2. Choose 🧩 Build (drag sound tiles to spell each word), ✏️ Write (trace each word), or Build + write (build the word, then trace it).
  3. Press Go. When tracing, each word gets the starting dots as help; to change that, tap ☰ at the top right.
  4. The sentence is read aloud. Each word is traced on its own, with the whole sentence shown above so it's clear where you are.
  5. When a sentence is finished it's read aloud again, and the next one starts by itself.

The different levels of help

🪄 Wand: a guide shows the path, the direction and where to start each stroke.
✨ Dots (default setting): only the starting point of each stroke is shown.
✏️ On their own: no help, but after two taps in the wrong place the starting dot appears.

There's no failing and no timer. Letters are formed top-down, with round letters going anticlockwise. Capital letters, full stops, question marks, commas and apostrophes are traced too.

Tips

Works best on an iPad, with an Apple Pencil if you have one. It's worth writing the same sentences on paper afterwards.

When building, words are split into sounds automatically (sh, ch, igh, ow, tch and so on). To split a word your own way, put dots between the sounds: b.r.ow.n or h.o.t.h.ou.se. The dots don't show to the learner.

In the ☰ menu you can hide the word while building (so it's spelled from hearing it, which needs Voice on) and turn the extra look-alike tiles on or off.

Roadmap

Auto-generate word chains: a button that writes practice sentences for the phonics concept you choose, such as short vowel words, "sh" and "ch", or this week's spelling pattern, so there's no need to come up with sentences yourself.

This would need a subscription to cover the AI costs.

Better voices: automatically use the most natural voice installed on the iPad, with a short guide for teachers on downloading a better one. Later, a natural-sounding voice that runs on the device or a cloud voice included with the subscription.

Play the word: a button that says the word, then each sound, then blends it back together, with an option to hide the written word so building becomes listening and spelling.

Pure sounds: recorded audio for each sound (/sss/, not "suh"), in a New Zealand accent, so sounding out models exactly what's taught in class.

Made by Dan Milward

I built this during my teaching practicum. It was designed and developed to support classrooms and teachers working with Tier 2 and Tier 3 students who need some extra scaffolding.

Privacy

No student data leaves this device. Ever. There are no accounts, no ads and no tracking.
